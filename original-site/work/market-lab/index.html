VVDexOps — market microstructure in public

A live, no-account platform that makes candles, technical indicators and the order-flow tools usually sold for €40–200 a month free for the public. Alongside them, M6E publishes its live BTC 15-minute research call and complete downloadable history — wins, losses and failed tests.

Two products in one public platform

I originally needed professional indicators and order-flow data for my own prediction research, but the available platforms were expensive or locked behind subscriptions. So I built the infrastructure myself. Anyone can now open VVDexOps without an account and chart BTC, ETH or SOL across 1m, 5m, 15m and 1h candles with EMA 9/21/50, Bollinger Bands, VWAP, RSI 14 and MACD. These are computed in the browser from the same bars the platform publishes, so the lines are reproducible rather than decorative.

Once the platform was working, I opened it to the public. The same infrastructure powers M6E, my experimental BTC 15-minute research engine. It records each live research call before settlement and exposes the result cycle by cycle. Visitors can watch the current call, compare it with the live Kalshi market and download the entire history.

The market, order by order

Beyond ordinary indicators, the live board adds footprint charts, volume-at-price, a 100ms order-book ladder, a configurable whale-trade tape, cumulative volume delta, cross-venue pricing, funding, open interest and forced liquidations. This is the market-microstructure tier that normally costs €40–200 a month or sits behind a signup form.

BTC, ETH and SOL stream from Binance, with Kraken providing an independent venue for divergence checks. Indicators are computed in the browser from the same public bars, so the displayed EMA, Bollinger, VWAP and RSI lines can be reproduced rather than taken on faith.

Free public market instruments on one side. A live, falsifiable research engine on the other.

M6E predicts every BTC 15-minute cycle

Every 15 minutes, Kalshi opens a market asking whether Bitcoin will finish above a specified strike. Five minutes into that cycle, M6E combines momentum and trend, mean reversion, order flow, liquidity events, derivatives and market context into one public call: UP, DOWN or ABSTAIN. The call is fixed at commitment and cannot change while the cycle settles.

At the public snapshot on 22 July 2026, M6E was correct on 69.6% of 670 committed calls. But the market's average implied probability at commitment was 69.8%. After the fee, measured edge was −0.017. So the direction signal is real and near 70% accurate, but it has not yet beaten the price required to trade it. That gap—not a flattering hit rate—is the active research problem.

69.6% directional accuracy. 69.8% market-implied odds. The remaining problem is beating the price after fees.

A record that resists rewriting

Predictions are written into an append-only forward ledger before their outcomes are known. Every call links to the previous one through a SHA-256 hash chain, and the full record is downloadable as CSV and JSON. Visitors can recompute the chain themselves; losing calls and refuted ideas are not filtered out.

The same page shows the live M6E call beside the exact market it is trying to beat: direction, conviction tier, probability, strike, spot at commitment, market ask and time remaining. At the same snapshot, 1,028 predictions were chained publicly, with 1,012 already settled.

The public product is isolated from the research system. Published snapshots move outward through a constrained, one-way path; the public server has no credential back into the research environment. The live browser layer connects directly to public exchange streams, so public traffic does not pass through or compete with the research pipeline.

Anyone can watch the current cycle, inspect every prior call and download the complete history—without trusting a screenshot.

Engineering decisions worth defending

Data exports are read-only and time-bounded. Every panel displays freshness instead of implying it. Indicator inputs are public and reproducible. A slow aggregation was cut from seconds to milliseconds by reading the database plan and changing the time-bucketing path. Sign conventions were verified independently because a reversed delta can still look plausible while invalidating every order-flow conclusion.

What it proves

Not profitability. VVDexOps proves the engineering and research discipline behind a live data product: real-time ingestion, market-microstructure visualisation, isolated publishing, reproducible measurements and falsifiable evaluation. "No edge yet" remains a valid result; now anyone can inspect why.
