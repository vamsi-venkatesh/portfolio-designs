01

The problem

Forecasts usually arrive as one confident number, with no way to see how it was picked or whether its ranges hold.

02

The plan

Make every step checkable: rules written down before the results, a simple baseline the model has to beat, and a person who approves.

03

What I built

01 / 03

Fifteen steps, each with one job: load and check the data, race seven models, pick by a written rule, test the ranges, approve, issue, read outside signals, write the decision paper

02 / 03

An outside-signal screen over a 75,884-signal catalogue with placebo and false-discovery control: on German industry, 5,022 tests and one survivor

03 / 03

Public runs are closed by default: the page shows one saved run with its receipt, and the owner approves who can sign in

04

How I know it works

A complex model must beat the best simple baseline by 5%
The untouched final periods are looked at once
Ranges that fail their test are withheld
Nothing is issued without a person's approval
